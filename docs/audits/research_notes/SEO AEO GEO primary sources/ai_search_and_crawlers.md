## Google: AI Overviews, AI Mode, required markup, and controls

### Takeaway
Google's own pages say there is nothing special to add: to be cited in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page needs to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet in Google Search. No llms.txt, AI-only markup, Markdown copies, "chunking" or special schema.org is needed, and Google Search ignores llms.txt. Google has three separate controls. Googlebot robots.txt rules and the snippet/index controls (`nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, `noindex`) govern Search, including its AI features. The new Search Console **"Search generative AI" control** (announced 3 June 2026, rolled out worldwide 31 August 2026) removes a whole site or property from AI Overviews, AI Mode and Discover's generative features without affecting classic ranking. **Google-Extended** only covers Gemini-model training and grounding outside Search. It does not affect Search inclusion or ranking.

### Inferences
- For Google, a server-rendered page that is indexable, has no `nosnippet`/`max-snippet:0`, and is not excluded in the Search Console control is eligible for AI Overviews and AI Mode. Nothing more is required. Auditors should check four separate layers: (1) robots.txt for `Googlebot`, plus CDN/WAF rules that may block Googlebot (for example a Cloudflare Training = "Block" policy, which blocks Googlebot since 2026-09-15; see the Cloudflare section); (2) `noindex`/`nosnippet`/`max-snippet`/`data-nosnippet` in the *served* HTML; (3) Settings > Search generative AI in Search Console; (4) the `Google-Extended` token. Only the first three affect Search AI features.
- Disallowing `Google-Extended` does **not** remove a site from AI Overviews or AI Mode (Google says it covers Gemini Apps/Vertex training and grounding, not Search). Blocking `Googlebot` removes the site from Search entirely.
- Because `data-nosnippet` "may happen both before and after rendering", placing it in server-rendered HTML (not added by client JS) is the only reliable form.
- The "AI features and your website" page (last updated 2025-12-10) predates the 2026 Search Console control and still calls Googlebot robots.txt "the control", which is now incomplete. The newer guide (2026-07-10) and the Help page are the current statements.

### Gaps
- Google does not document how AI Overviews/AI Mode choose which passage of a page to cite. Nothing found on whether hidden (`hidden` attribute, collapsed accordion) text is treated differently for AI features than for classic snippets; see the JavaScript/hidden-content section.
- The Search Console control is property-level. Google documents no page-level AI-only opt-out other than `nosnippet`/`data-nosnippet`/`max-snippet`, which also affect classic snippets.

## Microsoft Bing / Copilot

### Takeaway
Bing's rewritten Webmaster Guidelines now cover Bing search, Copilot and "grounding API results" together. Microsoft says the same crawling, indexing and ranking foundation feeds all three. For AI answers its controls are: `NOINDEX` removes a URL from Bing, Copilot and grounding; `NOARCHIVE` keeps content out of Copilot and grounding answers; `NOCACHE` limits Copilot to URL, title and snippet; `NOSNIPPET`/`data-nosnippet` stop captions and AI summaries and may weaken Copilot citations. Bing says to avoid "hiding critical content behind client-side rendering". It recommends IndexNow and accurate `lastmod` for freshness in AI answers. It says structured data "may support clearer grounding" if it matches visible content. Bing Webmaster Tools has an AI Performance (citations) report since February 2026. I found no Microsoft statement on llms.txt.

## Do AI crawlers execute JavaScript? Late-streamed `hidden` content, accordions and tabs

### Takeaway
Only Google (Googlebot's rendering service) and Apple (Applebot "may render") document rendering. Anthropic documents that its API web-fetch tool does not support JavaScript-rendered sites. Bing warns against hiding critical content behind client-side rendering. Independent tests agree: the OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity fetchers read raw HTML (Vercel/MERJ, Dec 2024), and live in-chat fetches by the major US assistants, including Gemini, read raw HTML (2025–2026 tests). So for a server-rendered page, what counts is what is in the raw HTML bytes, in order. No public test was found of React out-of-order streaming specifically, i.e. content placed late in the document inside `<div hidden id="S:…">` and moved by an inline script. The text is in the raw HTML, so HTML-only fetchers can see it. But (1) it sits out of reading order, after the shell; (2) extractors that drop `hidden` nodes would discard it, and the widely used Mozilla Readability algorithm does exactly that; (3) size or token truncation cuts the end of the document first. CSS-hidden accordion/tab content in the HTML was read by most assistants in one 2026 test. Content loaded only after a click is not read by Google and not by HTML-only fetchers.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Vendor documentation**
- [VENDOR DOC – Google] "Once Google's resources allow, a headless Chromium renders the page and executes the JavaScript." and "Keep in mind that server-side or pre-rendering is still a great idea because it makes your website faster for users and crawlers, and not all bots can run JavaScript." — [Understand the JavaScript SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics), Last updated 2026-03-04 UTC.
- [VENDOR DOC – Google] Rendering is not guaranteed: "Google typically renders pages in order to index them, however rendering is not guaranteed." — [Robots meta tag spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (2026-03-24).
- [VENDOR DOC – Google] Size cutoff: "When crawling for Google Search, Googlebot crawls the first 2MB of a supported file type … Once the cutoff limit is reached, Googlebot stops the fetch and only sends the already downloaded part of the file for indexing consideration. The file size limit is applied on the uncompressed data." — [Googlebot](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/googlebot) (2026-02-03).
- [VENDOR DOC – Google] Tabs/accordions and interaction: "You can have a different design on mobile to maximize user experience (for example, moving content into accordions or tabs); just make sure that the content is equivalent to the desktop site" and "Instead of removing content, consider moving content into accordions or tabs to save space." - [MEASUREMENT – secondary, reported second-hand] A decoy test (summer 2026, run by Andre Alpar's team and reported by Advanced Web Ranking). Each of twelve assistants got its own never-linked URL. Only four China-based assistants and one Europe-based assistant ran the JavaScript. The reported interpretation: Gemini's answers can reflect pages Googlebot already rendered for the index ("the index lane"), while its cold live fetch read raw HTML ("the live-answer lane"). — [Trust the Log, Not the Chatbot: What AI Search Crawlers Actually Read](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/blog/what-ai-search-crawlers-read), Gianluca Fiorelli, Advanced Web Ranking blog, Aug 20, 2026. The primary experiment write-up was not retrieved.

- [MEASUREMENT – secondary] Writesonic test (published 2026-03-30): one page with "62 unique hidden codes, each planted in a different location in the HTML", read by six assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and two others). - [CODE EVIDENCE – open-source extractor, not an AI vendor] Mozilla Readability, a widely reused "reader mode" main-content extractor, removes nodes it judges invisible. `_isProbablyVisible(node)` returns false when `node.style.display == "none"`, `node.style.visibility == "hidden"`, `node.hasAttribute("hidden")`, or `aria-hidden="true"`. ### Inferences
- **Out-of-order Suspense streaming (content in a late `<div hidden id="S:…">`, moved by inline `$RC`-style scripts).** For **Googlebot (Search, AI Overviews, AI Mode)** this is likely fine: Google renders with headless Chromium, the swap script runs, and Google grounds on its rendered index. The caveats are that rendering "is not guaranteed" and the 2MB uncompressed HTML cutoff. Late segments plus the inline component payload sit at the end of the byte stream, so on very heavy pages they are the first content lost. For **HTML-only AI fetchers** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, CCBot, and Gemini's cold live fetch per the 2026 test), the text is present but (1) appears after the footer or shell, cut off from the heading or section it belongs to, (2) is wrapped in an element with the `hidden` attribute, which Readability-style extractors delete outright, and (3) is the first thing lost when a fetcher truncates to a token or byte budget (Anthropic's API tool documents truncation). Exactly how OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity extract text is undocumented, so the outcome can only be established by testing.
- In the framework version in this repo, the user agents of OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity and Common Crawl are **not** on the "HTML-limited bots" list, so they get the normal streamed (out-of-order) response, not the fully formed document served to Bingbot, Applebot and Google's special crawlers. Adding their tokens to `htmlLimitedBots` is the framework's own mechanism for serving them blocking HTML. Note the framework's caveat that content "can still stream" with Cache Components, and the possible cloaking concern if bot and user HTML differ materially (Bing lists cloaking as abuse); the intent here would be identical content in a different order. This is an inference to test, not a vendor recommendation for AI bots.
- **Accordions and tabs.** Content present in the server HTML and hidden only by CSS was read by 5–6 of 6 assistants in the 2026 test, and Google indexes accordion/tab content that is in the page. The risks are: (a) content fetched only after a click (Google: "won't load content that requires user interactions"; HTML-only fetchers never click); (b) inactive tab panels or collapsed regions marked with the HTML `hidden` attribute or `aria-hidden="true"`, which Readability-style extractors drop (untested publicly for AI fetchers); (c) Google's own guidance that "Read more" deep links need immediately visible content. A native `<details>` element's collapsed content carries no `hidden` attribute, so Readability-style filters keep it. That is an inference from the code, not a tested AI-fetcher result.
- Practical audit test, following the measurements' method: fetch each URL with the actual AI user-agent strings (`OAI-SearchBot`, `ChatGPT-User`, `Claude-SearchBot`, `Claude-User`, `PerplexityBot`, `Perplexity-User`, `GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `CCBot`). Check that the primary text appears in the raw response, where it sits relative to its heading, whether it is inside a `hidden` element, and the total uncompressed byte size.

### Gaps
- No vendor or independent test was found that specifically measures how OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity or Microsoft fetchers treat text inside an HTML `hidden`-attribute element, or React out-of-order streaming segments. This is the key open question for streamed pages.
- No vendor documents its HTML-to-text extraction pipeline, byte or size limits (other than Google's 2MB/15MB and Anthropic's API `max_content_tokens`), or whether `aria-hidden`/`hidden` content is discarded.
- The 2026 decoy test is known only through Advanced Web Ranking's summary. The searchVIU and Writesonic tests are single-page experiments, and vendor pipelines change often.

## Cloudflare: AI crawler controls, defaults, and the 2026 change that lets "Block" hit Googlebot/Bingbot/Applebot

### Takeaway
Cloudflare's controls changed in stages. **Sep 2024:** one-click "Block AI bots". **1 Jul 2025:** managed robots.txt, and new domains are asked during sign-up whether to allow AI crawlers ("block … by default" framing). **24 Sep 2025:** Content Signals Policy (`search`, `ai-input`, `ai-train`). **1 Jul 2026:** behaviour-based policies (Search, Agent, Training) replace the single toggle. **15 Sep 2026:** "Block" and "Block on pages with ads" for **Training** now also block **mixed-use crawlers, explicitly Googlebot, Bingbot and Applebot**, i.e. search, not just AI. The new "Disallow AI Training" setting is the one that stops training while keeping Google, Bing and Apple search crawling; for Bing it does not yet convey a no-training signal. The settings live under **Security > Settings > (filter: Bot traffic) > AI Crawler and Agent access policies** for each zone. Robots.txt publishing is now "Bot Preference Sync", which replaces "Managed robots.txt". The current docs say new domains default to Allow for all three behaviours, while the 15 Sep 2026 post says ad-monetised new domains are offered a stricter preset. An auditor must read a zone's actual settings: a Training "Block" there blocks Googlebot and therefore Google Search, AI Overviews and AI Mode.

### Inferences
- **Highest-risk audit check:** on a Cloudflare zone, Security > Settings > AI Crawler and Agent access policies > **Training = Block** or **Block on pages with ads** now blocks Googlebot, Bingbot and Applebot at the edge (from 2026-09-15). That removes the site from Google Search, including AI Overviews and AI Mode, from Bing and Copilot, and from Apple search. The robots.txt can look perfectly permissive while the edge returns blocks. "Disallow AI Training" is the setting that keeps search.
- The 2026-07-01 changelog and blog predicted that new domains would get Training/Agent blocked on ad pages by default. The 2026-09-15 post and the Nov 2026 docs describe "Allow" defaults with an ads-based preset offered at onboarding. Treat the 15 Sep post and current docs as authoritative and verify the zone's actual setting.
- Search-category bots (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot) and Agent-category fetchers (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User) are governed separately. Blocking "Agent" stops live in-chat fetches, which some assistants use to cite pages.
- Verified status no longer guarantees access (since 2026-07-01). Bot Fight Mode can still challenge some AI bots (per Perplexity).

### Gaps
- Cloudflare's docs do not list, in one place, every crawler it classifies as "mixed-use"; the blog names Googlebot, Bingbot and Applebot.
- The current doc says new-domain defaults are "Allow (do not block)", while the 2026-09-15 post describes ad-dependent presets offered at onboarding. These may be consistent (default vs offered preset), but the docs do not state it explicitly.
- "AI Labyrinth" and the "Block AI Bots" legacy page behaviour after deprecation were not researched.

## llms.txt

### Takeaway
llms.txt is a 2024 proposal, revised as "v2" in August 2026, for a Markdown file at `/llms.txt` (or any subpath), plus `.md` copies of pages, to guide agents at inference time. **Google Search officially says it does not use llms.txt and ignores it.** Google's Chrome team, separately, added an optional llms.txt check to Lighthouse's experimental "Agentic Browsing" audits (doc dated 2026-05-05). No AI-search vendor among OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Microsoft or Apple has officially stated that its crawler or answer engine reads llms.txt. AI labs do publish llms.txt files for their own developer docs. Server-log evidence (Ahrefs, 137K domains, May 2026) shows 97% of published llms.txt files received zero requests. AI retrieval bots barely fetched them.

### Cited Findings
- [PROPOSAL – primary] "A proposal to standardise on using an `/llms.txt` file to provide information to help agents use a website." Author Jeremy Howard, "Published September 3, 2024", "Modified August 10, 2026". "We propose adding a `/llms.txt` markdown file to websites to provide LLM-friendly content. The file can be placed at the site root, or at any path within it, covering the pages under that path." … "We furthermore propose that pages with information that agents might need provide a clean markdown version of those pages at the same URL as the original page, either with `.md` appended (`page.html.md`) or with the extension replaced by `.md` (`page.md`)." … "To help clients find these files, we recommend using standard link relations: `rel="alternate" type="text/markdown"` points to the markdown version of a page, and `rel="describedby"` points to the llms.txt file that covers it." On use: "robots.txt lets automated tools know what access to a site is considered acceptable … llms.txt information is instead used on demand, when an agent needs information about a topic while assisting a user. Our expectation was that llms.txt would mainly be useful for inference rather than training". The author's adoption claim (not independently verified): "thousands of sites publish an llms.txt file … and Chrome's Lighthouse audits sites for one as part of its agentic browsing checks. The AI labs themselves publish llms.txt files for their own developer docs: OpenAI, Anthropic, and Gemini." — [The /llms.txt file, v2](https://llmstxt.org/), retrieved 2026-11-12.
- [VENDOR DOC – Google Search] "LLMS.txt files and other "special" markup: You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search (including its generative AI capabilities), as Google Search itself doesn't use them. Note that Google may discover, crawl, and index many kinds of files in addition to HTML on a website: this doesn't mean that the file is treated in a special way. It's completely fine if you decide to create and maintain LLMS.txt files (or other similar files) for other services or systems that use these files. Doing so will neither harm nor help your site's visibility or rankings in Google Search, as Google Search ignores them." — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), Last updated 2026-07-10 UTC.
- [VENDOR DOC – Google Chrome/Lighthouse] "The `llms.txt` file is an emerging convention used to provide a machine-readable summary of a website's content, specifically designed for LLMs and AI agents. Without this file, agents may spend more time crawling the site to understand its high-level structure and primary content." and "If the file is not provided by the server (resulting in a 404), the audit is marked as Not Applicable (N/A), as providing the file is optional at the moment." — [Lighthouse: llms.txt (Agentic browsing audits)](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/llms-txt), Last updated 2026-05-05 UTC.
- [VENDOR DOC – absence] The OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity and Apple crawler pages, the Bing Webmaster Guidelines and the Cloudflare docs read for these notes contain no statement that the vendor's crawler or answer engine reads or uses llms.txt ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), [Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler), [Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers), [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119829), [Bing](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)). Cloudflare itself publishes llms.txt files for its developer docs (e.g. `/ai-crawl-control/llms.txt` is linked from its docs pages). That is a publishing choice, not a crawler statement.
- [MEASUREMENT – secondary] Ahrefs log study (server logs of 137,210 domains, May 2026): "28% of the 137K domains using Ahrefs Web Analytics publish an llms.txt file." "97% of those files received zero traffic in May 2026. Nothing fetched them at all." "96% of the requests that did reach llms.txt files came from bots." "19.5% of fetches came from named AI tools (of the 3% of files that weren't ignored). GPTBot is top and Claude-Code is second, ahead of every AI search and assistant bot." "Zero requests came from AI bots for llms.txt files that don't exist. They never go looking." "OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, and Claude's search crawler combined made only a couple of hundred fetches across thousands of sites." "The Chrome Lighthouse llms.txt audit produced roughly 1 in 1,000 fetches." Caveat in the study: Ahrefs' customers "skew more technical and SEO-aware than the web at large, so treat the 28% adoption figure as an upper bound." — [We Analyzed 137K Sites: 97% of llms.txt Files Never Get Read](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/), Ahrefs, published 2026-06-15, modified 2026-11-06.
- [OPINION – secondary] The same article reports Google's John Mueller saying llms.txt is "not done for search" and a "temporary crutch, perhaps to save some tokens" for AI coding tools. This is a social-media remark quoted by Ahrefs, not documentation. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/).

### Inferences
- llms.txt is not a ranking or citation lever for Google (explicitly ignored). There is no evidence it is one for OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity or Microsoft answer engines. Its demonstrated use is by coding agents and developer-doc consumers. For a consumer or local-business site it is optional; Lighthouse treats a 404 as "N/A", not a failure. Serving a broken (5xx) `/llms.txt` would fail the Lighthouse check.
- Serving bot-only Markdown copies that differ from the HTML risks being treated as cloaking under Bing's guidelines. The llms.txt v2 proposal's `.md` mirrors should be content-identical.

### Gaps
- No primary statement from OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Microsoft or Apple saying their crawlers or answer engines do or do not read llms.txt was found.
- Ahrefs' data is one month (May 2026) from its own customer base. Other log studies found online are single-site anecdotes and were not used.

## Vendor guidance on structured data (JSON-LD) and AI answers

### Takeaway
Google says structured data is **not required** for AI Overviews or AI Mode and there is **no special schema**. It recommends keeping schema for rich results, and only if it matches visible text. Robots snippet limits do not restrict structured data, and structured data in a `data-nosnippet` block stays usable. Bing says structured data "may support clearer grounding" but guarantees nothing, and misleading markup may be ignored. Apple documents one AI-relevant schema property: `isAccessibleForFree: false` keeps a page out of AI answer context. OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity publish no structured-data guidance. Independent tests found live AI fetchers do not read JSON-LD-only facts. One matched study found adding JSON-LD produced no meaningful citation uplift.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR DOC – Google] "Overfocusing on structured data: Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add. However, it's a good idea to continue using it as part of your overall SEO strategy, as it helps with being eligible for rich results on Google Search." — [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) (2026-07-10). And: "Making sure your structured data matches the visible text on the page" / "There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add." — [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) (2025-12-10).
- [VENDOR DOC – Google] "don't add structured data about information that is not visible to the user, even if the information is accurate." and "Google can read JSON-LD data when it is dynamically injected into the page's contents, such as by JavaScript code". — [Introduction to structured data markup](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data), Last updated 2025-12-10 UTC.
- [VENDOR DOC – Google] Snippet controls vs schema: "Robots `meta` tag limitations don't affect the use of that structured data, with the exception of `article.description` and the `description` values for structured data specified for other creative works." The `max-snippet` limit "does not apply in cases where a publisher has separately granted permission for use of content. For instance, if the publisher supplies content in the form of in-page structured data". — [Robots meta tag spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (2026-03-24).
- [VENDOR DOC – Microsoft] "Use Structured Data Accurately: Structured data may support clearer grounding but does not guarantee visibility or grounding traffic. Markup must accurately reflect visible content." and under abuse: "Misleading Structured Data: Structured data must accurately represent visible content. Markup that is irrelevant, inaccurate, or misleading may be ignored and can affect trust and eligibility for enhanced features." — [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a). Microsoft Advertising blog: "Schema is a type of code that helps search engines and AI systems understand your content." — [Optimizing Your Content for Inclusion in AI Search Answers](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers) (Oct 08, 2025).
- [VENDOR DOC – Apple] "Applebot supports the schema.org `isAccessibleForFree` property to identify pages that are behind a paywall, metered access, or subscription." … "Pages marked `isAccessibleForFree: false` are eligible to appear in search results, but Applebot will not use that content as additional context when AI models are used to generate output for display in Apple products and services. This signal applies at the page level. Section-level markup using `hasPart` is not supported." — [About Applebot](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119829) (Published Date: September 04, 2026).
- [VENDOR DOC – absence] OpenAI's, Anthropic's and Perplexity's crawler pages say nothing about structured data ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), [Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler), [Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers)).
- [MEASUREMENT – secondary] searchVIU (30 Oct 2025): "No system named the price that was only in the JSON-LD." The same held for JSON-LD injected via JavaScript, hidden microdata, and hidden RDFa plus conflicting JSON-LD. "With two prices, no system named either of them in the test." Its own caveat: "The test does not answer whether Google AI Overviews or Bing Copilot use schema from the index." — [searchVIU: Schema markup and AI](https://www.searchviu.com/en/guides/schema-markup-ai/) (Last updated October 15, 2026).
- [MEASUREMENT – secondary] Writesonic (2026-03-30): "JSON-LD, meta descriptions, OG tags scored 0/6. The `<title>` tag is the only metadata that reliably reaches AI assistants (5/6)." — [Writesonic AI crawler study](https://writesonic.com/blog/ai-crawler-study-what-llms-see-on-your-website).
- [MEASUREMENT – secondary] Ahrefs matched study: "We tracked 1,885 web pages that added JSON-LD schema between August 2025 and March 2026, matched them against 4,000 control pages, and measured citation changes across Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, and ChatGPT. Adding schema produced no major uplift in citations on any platform." Reported effects: Google AIO "−4.6%" ("Small but statistically significant decline relative to matched controls"), AI Mode "+2.4%" and ChatGPT "+2.2%" (both "Statistically indistinguishable from zero"). Correlational starting point: "AI cited pages were almost three times more likely to have JSON-LD than non-cited pages." — [We Tracked 1,885 Pages Adding Schema. AI Citations Barely Moved.](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/), Ahrefs, May 11, 2026 (modified 2026-06-09).
- [OPINION – secondary] Bing's Fabrice Canel reportedly said at SMX Munich (March 2025) that "schema markup helps Microsoft's LLMs understand your content" (attendee quote via LinkedIn) — [Search Engine Roundtable, 2025-03-20](https://www.seroundtable.com/schema-llms-copilot-bing-microsoft-39093.html).

### Inferences
- For citation by AI answer engines, put every fact that matters (prices, hours, addresses, ratings) in **visible, server-rendered HTML text**. JSON-LD is a supporting, index-time signal (Google rich results; Bing says "may support clearer grounding"), not something live AI fetchers read.
- Schema that does not match the visible text is a liability for both Google and Bing. Conflicting values led to *neither* value being reported in the searchVIU test.
- `isAccessibleForFree: false` has an AI-specific effect at Apple: it removes the page from AI answer context. Avoid it on free pages.

### Gaps
- No vendor states whether its *index* (as opposed to live fetch) feeds JSON-LD into AI answer generation, beyond Bing's hedged "may support clearer grounding".
- OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity have no published structured-data guidance.
