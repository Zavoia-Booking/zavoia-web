## 1. Title links: how Google chooses them, sources besides `<title>`, length, boilerplate and repetition

### Takeaway
Google generates title links automatically from nine listed sources: `<title>`, the main visual title, headings such as `<h1>`, `og:title`, other large and prominent text, other page text, anchor text on the page, the text of links pointing to the page, and `WebSite` structured data. Google states there is "no limit" on `<title>` length but truncates to the device width. It warns against keyword repetition, boilerplate and micro-boilerplate titles, and recommends putting the site name once, at the beginning or end. For domain-level site names, Google "may omit the site name from the title link" when it repeats the site name already shown in the result.

### Cited Findings
- "Google's generation of title links on the Google Search results page is completely automated and takes into account both the content of a page and references to it that appear on the web. The goal of the title link is to best represent and describe each result." — [Google Search Central: Influencing your title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- "Google Search uses the following sources to automatically determine title links: Content in `<title>` elements; Main visual title shown on the page; Heading elements, such as `<h1>` elements; Content in `og:title` `meta` tags; Other content that's large and prominent through the use of style treatments; Other text contained in the page; Anchor text on the page; Text within links that point to the page; `WebSite` structured data" (bulleted list on the page) — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- `og:title` became a documented source on 26 Aug 2024: "Added `og:title` to the list of title link sources." / "Google Search can use content within `og:title` `meta` tags to automatically generate title links." — [Google Search Central: Latest documentation updates (changelog), August 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) (LU 2026-11-09 · R 2026-11-12)
- "Make sure every page on your site has a title specified in the `<title>` element." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Length: "Write descriptive and concise text for your `<title>` elements. Avoid vague descriptors like "Home" for your home page, or "Profile" for a specific person's profile. Also avoid unnecessarily long or verbose text in your `<title>` elements. While there's no limit on how long a `<title>` element can be, the title link is truncated in Google Search results as needed, typically to fit the device width." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Repetition and keyword stuffing: "Avoid keyword stuffing. It's sometimes helpful to have a few descriptive terms in the `<title>` element, but there's no reason to have the same words or phrases appear multiple times. Title text like "Foobar, foo bar, foobars, foo bars" doesn't help the user, and this kind of keyword stuffing can make your results look spammy to Google and to users." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Boilerplate: "Avoid repeated or boilerplate text in `<title>` elements. It's important to have distinct text that describes the content of the page in the `<title>` element for each page on your site. [...] Long text in the `<title>` element that varies by only a single piece of information ("boilerplate" titles) is also bad [...] One solution is to dynamically update the `<title>` element to better reflect the actual content of the page." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Brand placement: "Brand your titles concisely. The `<title>` element on your site's home page is a reasonable place to include some additional information about your site. [...] But displaying that text in the `<title>` element of every single page on your site will look repetitive if several pages from your site are returned for the same query. In this case, consider including just your site name at the beginning or end of each `<title>` element, separated from the rest of the text with a delimiter such as a hyphen, colon, or pipe" — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Main title: "Make it clear which text is the main title for the page. Google looks at various sources when creating title links, including the main visual title, heading elements, and other large and prominent text, and it can be confusing if multiple headings carry the same visual weight and prominence. Consider ensuring that your main title is distinctive from other text on a page and stands out as being the most prominent on the page (for example, using a larger font, putting the title text in the first visible `<h1>` element on the page, etc)." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Blocked pages: "If we don't have access to the content on your page, we will rely on off-page content to generate the title link, such as anchor text from other sites. To prevent a URL from being indexed, you can use the `noindex` rule." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Language and script: "Use the same language and writing system (meaning, the script or alphabet for a given language) as the primary content on your pages. [...] If Google determines that a `<title>` element does not match the writing system or language of the page's primary content, we may choose a different text as the title link." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- "Avoid including flight price information in `<title>` elements." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Why the title link can differ: "If we've detected an issue on the page, we may try to generate an improved title link from anchors, on-page text, or other sources." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Common issues Google adjusts for, as named on the page: "Half-empty `<title>` elements", "Obsolete `<title>` elements", "Inaccurate `<title>` elements", "Micro-boilerplate text in `<title>` elements", "No clear main title" ("If Google Search detects that there are multiple large, prominent headings, it may use the first heading as the text for the title link."), "Mismatch of writing system or language used in `<title>` elements". — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Site-name duplication: "Duplication of the site name in the `<title>` element — In the case of domain-level site names, Google may omit the site name from the title link, if it's repetitive with the site name that's already shown in the search result." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Recrawl: "Keep in mind that Google has to recrawl and reprocess the page to notice updates to these sources, which may take a few days to a few weeks." — [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Image landing pages: "We use a number of different sources for this information, including information in the `title` and `meta` tags for each page." — [Google image SEO best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images) (LU 2026-03-02 · R 2026-11-12)

### Inferences
- Google has no explicit rule about a brand suffix repeated twice within one `<title>` (for example "Page | Brand | Brand"). Three Google statements bear on it: "no reason to have the same words or phrases appear multiple times", "consider including just your site name at the beginning or end", and the "Duplication of the site name" behaviour. A doubled brand therefore goes against Google's guidance and wastes truncation space. Google says it may drop a site name that repeats the displayed site name, but does not promise to.
- Character limits of 50–60 characters are third-party heuristics. Google states only "no limit" plus device-width truncation.
- Google lists `og:title` as an input, so a mismatch between `og:title`, `<title>` and the visible `<h1>` gives Google conflicting candidates. Google's "make it clear which text is the main title" guidance argues for keeping them consistent.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no pixel or character truncation width.
- The changelog lists no 2025–2026 change to the title-link page (LU 2025-12-10 appears to be a site-wide refresh). The last substantive change I found was the addition of `og:title` on 26 Aug 2024.

## 2. Snippets: meta description use, length, and the `nosnippet` / `data-nosnippet` / `max-snippet` controls

### Takeaway
Google builds snippets "primarily" from page content and may use the meta description "when it describes the page better". Google states "There's no limit on how long a meta description can be"; snippets are truncated to the device width. `nosnippet`, `max-snippet:[n]` and `data-nosnippet` (valid only on `span`, `div` and `section`) control snippets. Since 2025 Google also says they apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode, including use as "direct input". Structured data is exempt from `max-snippet` except for `description` values of creative works. On 20 Apr 2026 Google added "read more" deep-link guidance.

### Cited Findings
- "A snippet is the description or summary part of search result on Google Search and other properties (for example, Google News). Google primarily uses the content on the page to automatically determine the appropriate snippet. We may also use descriptive information in the meta description element when it describes the page better than other parts of the content." — [Google Search Central: Control your snippets in search results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (LU 2026-04-20 · R 2026-11-12)
- "Snippets are designed to emphasize and preview the page content that best relates to a user's specific search. This means that Google Search might show different snippets for different searches." — [Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (LU 2026-04-20 · R 2026-11-12)
- Length: "There's no limit on how long a meta description can be, but the snippet is truncated in Google Search results as needed, typically to fit the device width." — [Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (LU 2026-04-20 · R 2026-11-12)
- Uniqueness: "Identical or similar descriptions on every page of a site aren't helpful when individual pages appear in search results. Wherever possible, create descriptions that accurately describe the specific page. Use site-level descriptions on the main home page or other aggregation pages, and use page-level descriptions everywhere else." — [Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (LU 2026-04-20 · R 2026-11-12)
- Programmatic descriptions: "programmatic generation of the descriptions can be appropriate and are encouraged. Good descriptions are human-readable and diverse." and "meta descriptions comprised of long strings of keywords don't give users a clear idea of the page's content, and are less likely to be displayed as a snippet." — [Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (LU 2026-04-20 · R 2026-11-12)
- Controls: "To prevent Google from displaying a snippet for your page in search results, use the `nosnippet` meta tag. To specify the maximum length for your snippets, use the `max-snippet:[number]` `meta` tag. You can also prevent certain parts of the page from being shown in a snippet by using the `data-nosnippet` attribute." — [Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (LU 2026-04-20 · R 2026-11-12)
- New on 20 Apr 2026, "read more" deep links: "Make sure content is immediately visible on the page to a human (and not hidden behind an expandable section or tabbed interface, for example)." / "Avoid using JavaScript to control the user's scroll position on page load" / "If you make history API calls or window.location.hash modifications on page load, make sure you don't remove the hash fragment from the URL, as this breaks deep linking behavior." — [Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (LU 2026-04-20 · R 2026-11-12); dated in [changelog, April 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) ("April 20 — Added a section about "read more" deep links")
- Earlier change, 5 Jan 2024: "Clarified in our documentation about snippets that the primary source of the snippet is the page content itself." / "The previous wording incorrectly implied that structured data and the meta description HTML element are the primary sources for snippets." — [changelog, January 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) (LU 2026-11-09 · R 2026-11-12)
- `nosnippet`: "Do not show a text snippet or video preview in the search results for this page. A static image thumbnail (if available) may still be visible, when it results in a better user experience. This applies to all forms of search results (at Google: web search, Google Images, Discover, AI Overviews, AI Mode) and will also prevent the content from being used as a direct input for AI Overviews and AI Mode." — [Google: Robots `meta` tag, `data-nosnippet`, and `X-Robots-Tag` specifications](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (LU 2026-03-24 · R 2026-11-12)
- `max-snippet:[number]`: "Use a maximum of [number] characters as a textual snippet for this search result. [...] This does not affect image or video previews. This applies to all forms of search results (such as Google web search, Google Images, Discover, Assistant, AI Overviews, AI Mode) and will also limit how much of the content may be used as a direct input for AI Overviews and AI Mode. However, this limit does not apply in cases where a publisher has separately granted permission for use of content. For instance, if the publisher supplies content in the form of in-page structured data [...] If you don't specify this rule, Google will choose the length of the snippet. Special values: `0`: No snippet is to be shown. Equivalent to `nosnippet`. `-1`: Google will choose the snippet length that it believes is most effective" — [Robots meta spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (LU 2026-03-24 · R 2026-11-12)
- Conflicting rules: "In the case of conflicting robots rules, the more restrictive rule applies. For example, if a page has both `max-snippet:50` and `nosnippet` rules, the `nosnippet` rule will apply." — [Robots meta spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (LU 2026-03-24 · R 2026-11-12)
- `data-nosnippet`: "You can designate textual parts of an HTML page not to be used as a snippet. This can be done on an HTML-element level with the `data-nosnippet` HTML attribute on `span`, `div`, and `section` elements. The `data-nosnippet` is considered a boolean attribute. As with all boolean attributes, any value specified is ignored." The page's example marks `<mytag data-nosnippet>` as "NOT VALID: not a span, div, or section". — [Robots meta spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (LU 2026-03-24 · R 2026-11-12)
- `data-nosnippet` and JavaScript: "extraction of `data-nosnippet` may happen both before and after rendering. To avoid uncertainty from rendering, do not add or remove the `data-nosnippet` attribute of existing nodes through JavaScript. When adding DOM elements through JavaScript, include the `data-nosnippet` attribute as necessary when initially adding the element to the page's DOM." — [Robots meta spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (LU 2026-03-24 · R 2026-11-12)
- Structured data versus snippet limits: "Robots `meta` tag limitations don't affect the use of that structured data, with the exception of `article.description` and the `description` values for structured data specified for other creative works. [...] Also note that structured data remains usable for search results when declared within a `data-nosnippet` element." — [Robots meta spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (LU 2026-03-24 · R 2026-11-12)
- Placement of robots meta tags, documented 24 Mar 2026: "Note: Google Search doesn't enforce placement of meta robots in the HTML head and will respect robots meta tags in the body section of an HTML document as well." — [Robots meta spec](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) (LU 2026-03-24 · R 2026-11-12). The changelog dates it to 24 Mar 2026 and adds: "The behavior didn't change but was previously undocumented." — [changelog, March 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
- AI features: "To limit the information shown from your pages in Search, use `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, or `noindex` controls." and "To be eligible to be shown as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, fulfilling the Search technical requirements. There are no additional technical requirements." — [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- New option listed 21 Oct 2026: "Use the Search generative AI control — Applicable: site-wide. You can manage your site's inclusion in generative AI features on Google Search, including AI Overviews and AI Mode, by using the Search generative AI control in Search Console." — [Google: Control what you share with Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/control-what-you-share) (LU 2026-10-21 · R 2026-11-12)
- Meta description in the supported-tags list: "Use this tag to provide a short description of the page. In some situations, this description is used in the snippet shown in search results." — [Google: `meta` tags and attributes that Google supports](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/special-tags) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)

### Inferences
- Meta description length targets such as "155–160 characters" are not Google rules. Google states no limit, and the description is used only "sometimes".
- `data-nosnippet` on any element other than `span`, `div` or `section` (for example `p`, `li`, `article` or custom elements) does not work for Google.
- Since 2025 `nosnippet` and `max-snippet` are also AI-input controls. Applying them site-wide to keep content out of AI features would also suppress classic snippets. Google requires snippet eligibility for AI supporting links.

### Gaps
- Google does not say how often, or by what criteria, it chooses the meta description over page text.
- No Google page I read says whether `og:description` is used for snippets. The snippet and supported-meta-tag pages do not mention it.

## 3. Site names: selection, `WebSite` properties, and home-page-only placement

### Takeaway
The site name is chosen automatically. `WebSite` structured data on the home page is "most important" for stating a preference; Google also considers `og:site_name`, `<title>`, headings and other home-page text. Required properties are `name` and `url`; `alternateName` (an ordered list) is recommended. Markup must be on the domain or subdomain root (no subdirectories, one name per site), and the home page must be crawlable. The Rich Results Test does not support site names.

### Cited Findings
- "When Google lists a page in search results, it shows the name of the site the page comes from. This is called the site name. Note that the site name is different from the per-page title links" — [Google Search Central: Provide a site name to Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Availability: "Site names are available in all languages where Google Search is available, on both mobile and desktop. Site names can appear for domain-level and subdomain-level sites" — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- How chosen: "Google's generation of site names on the Google Search results page is completely automated and takes into account content from a site's home page and references to it that appear on the web." / "To indicate your site name preference, add `WebSite` structured data to your home page. Our site name system will also consider content in `og:site_name`, `<title>`, heading elements, and other text on a home page. However, `WebSite` structured data is most important, if you want to specify a preference." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Choosing a name: "Use a concise, commonly-recognized name for your site [...] While there's no limit to how long a site name can be, long site names may be truncated on some devices." / "Avoid using a generic name." / "Use your site name consistently across your home page." / "our system generally won't use the same site name for two different sites that are global in nature." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- One name per site: "Currently, Google Search only supports one site name per site, where a site is defined by the domain or subdomain. Google Search does not support site names at the subdirectory level. Note that subdomain names starting with `www` or `m` are generally considered as being equivalent." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Home page only: "Structured data must be on the home page of a site: The `WebSite` structured data must be on the home page of the site. By home page, we mean the domain or subdomain level root URI. For example, `https://example.com` is the home page of the domain, while `https://example.com/de/index.html` isn't the home page." / "Note: If there's no structured data on the subdomain's home page, then the domain-level site name may be used for the subdomain as a fallback." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- "You don't need to include this markup on every page of your site; you only need to add this markup to the home page of your site." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Crawlability and duplicates: "The home page must be crawlable by Google: If we don't have access to the content on your home page because it is blocked, we may not be able to generate a site name." / "For sites with duplicate home pages [...] make sure that you're using the same structured data on all page duplicates, not just on the canonical page." / "If you already have `WebSite` structured data on your site, make sure that you nest the site name properties in the same node. In other words, avoid creating an additional `WebSite` structured data block on your home page if you can help it." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Required properties: "`name` — `Text` The name of the website. Make sure the name meets the guidelines for choosing your site name." / "`url` — `URL` The URL of the home page of the site. Set this to the canonical home page of your site's domain or subdomain." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Recommended property: "`alternateName` — `Text` The alternate name of the website (for example, if there's a commonly recognized acronym or shorter name for your site), if applicable. [...] You can list more than one alternative name. Specify them in order of your preference, with the most important one listed first." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Fallbacks: "Provide your domain or subdomain name as a backup option. [...] Your domain or subdomain needs to be in all lowercase (for example, `example.com` not `Example.com`) for our system to detect this as a site name preference." The page calls using the domain as `name` "a last-resort workaround option". — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Testing: "Validate your markup with a schema testing tool (for example, Schema Markup Validator) to make sure there are no syntax errors. Site names aren't supported in the Rich Results Test." — [Site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Organization link: "`name` — `Text` The name of your organization. Use the same `name` and `alternateName` that you're using for your site name." — [Google: Organization structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization) (LU 2026-09-08 · R 2026-11-12)
- Sitelinks search box, the other `WebSite` use: "we'll be removing this visual element starting on November 21, 2024. [...] While you can remove sitelinks search box structured data from your site, there's no need to do so. Unsupported structured data like this won't cause issues in Search, and won't trigger errors in Search Console reports. If you decide to remove sitelinks search box structured data, note that site names also uses a variation of `WebSite` structured data, which continues to be supported." — [Search Central Blog: Farewell, Sitelinks Search Box (21 Oct 2024)](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/10/sitelinks-search-box) (R 2026-11-12); docs removed 29 Nov 2024 per [changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)

### Inferences
- `WebSite` markup on inner pages does nothing for site names. A `potentialAction`/`SearchAction` inside `WebSite` no longer produces any Google feature, but it is harmless.
- Because `og:site_name` and `<title>` are secondary inputs, a home page whose `og:site_name`, `<title>` brand and `WebSite.name` disagree goes against "Use your site name consistently across your home page".
- Locale subdirectories (for example `/ro/`) cannot have their own site name. Only the root or a subdomain can.

### Gaps
- The changelog has no 2025–2026 changes to the site names page. The last substantive changes were in 2023 (global rollout on 7 Sep 2023; subdomains on 22 May 2023; "what to do if your preferred site name isn't selected" on 28 Jul 2023).
- Google does not document whether `inLanguage` or per-language site names are read.

## 4. Favicons: size, formats, `rel` values, crawlability, one per hostname

### Takeaway
Current rules (LU 2026-10-27): the favicon `<link>` goes on the home page; Google reads `rel="icon"` (and the legacy `shortcut icon`), `apple-touch-icon` and `apple-touch-icon-precomposed`; one favicon per hostname; Googlebot must crawl the home page and Googlebot-Image the file. The icon must be square and at least 8×8 px, with "larger than 48x48px" recommended. Formats are now listed explicitly as BMP, GIF, ICO, PNG, JPEG, PPM and TIFF; SVG is not in the list. The old "multiple of 48px" rule was dropped on 24 Oct 2024.

### Cited Findings
- "Add a `<link>` tag to the header of your home page with the following syntax: `<link rel="icon" href="/path/to/favicon.ico">`" — [Google Search Central: Define a favicon to show in search results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/favicon-in-search) (LU 2026-10-27 · R 2026-11-12)
- `rel` values Google reads: "`icon` — The icon that represents your site, as defined in the HTML standard. For historical reasons, we also support `shortcut icon`, which is an earlier, alternative version of `icon`." / "`apple-touch-icon` — An [...]-friendly icon that represents your site, per [...] developer documentation." / "`apple-touch-icon-precomposed` — An alternative icon for earlier versions of [...], per [...] developer documentation." — [Favicon](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/favicon-in-search) (LU 2026-10-27 · R 2026-11-12)
- `href`: "The URL can be a relative path (`/smile.ico`) or absolute path [...]. - Change on 28 Aug 2026: "Updated the favicon documentation to specifically list out the supported favicon file formats." / "To be clearer about which favicon file formats are supported by Google Search. - Older history: 24 Apr 2024 "Added definitions for each supported `rel` attribute value"; 23 Feb 2023 "Removed the hosting location requirement from the favicon documentation"; 2023: "both the favicon file and the home page of the site must be allowed for crawling by Googlebot-Image and Googlebot respectively." — [changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) (LU 2026-11-09 · R 2026-11-12)

### Inferences
- An SVG-only favicon (`<link rel="icon" type="image/svg+xml">` with no raster fallback) falls outside Google's explicit format list as of 28 Aug 2026. An auditor should expect at least one ICO or PNG favicon declared on the home page. Google says formats "haven't changed", which implies SVG was never formally supported despite the 2024 wording "(or SVG with a 1:1 (square) aspect ratio)". This is a documentation conflict: the 2024 and 2026 statements disagree on SVG.
- The "multiple of 48px" rule cited by many audit tools has not been Google's rule since 24 Oct 2024.
- Favicons are per hostname and site names are per domain or subdomain, so a locale subdirectory cannot have its own favicon or site name.

### Gaps
- Google does not say which icon wins when several `rel` values or sizes are declared, and does not mention dark-mode or `media`-query icons.
- The 28 Aug 2026 changelog explains the listing, but no page states whether the PNG/ICO requirement is enforced for already-indexed SVG favicons.

## 5. Images in Search: alt text, file names, captions, image sitemaps, `max-image-preview`, preferred image, licence/creator metadata

### Takeaway
Google indexes images only from `<img src>` (including inside `<picture>`), not CSS backgrounds. Supported formats are BMP, GIF, JPEG, PNG, WebP, SVG and AVIF. Alt text is "the most important attribute"; filenames give "very light clues"; nearby text and captions are used. Image sitemaps still work with only `<image:image>` and `<image:loc>` (caption, title, geo_location and license tags were deprecated in 2022). `max-image-preview` takes `none`, `standard` or `large`; Discover large images need `large` and at least 1200 px width. New on 2 Mar 2026: Google documents `primaryImageOfPage`, `image` on the main entity, and `og:image` as preferred-image sources. Licensable-badge metadata requires `contentUrl` plus one of `creator`, `creditText`, `copyrightNotice` or `license`; `license` is required for the badge.

## 6. Core Web Vitals: metrics, "good" thresholds, percentile, and the ranking role of page experience

### Takeaway
The current Core Web Vitals are LCP, INP and CLS. INP replaced FID in March 2024. "Good" means LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms and CLS ≤ 0.1, assessed at the 75th percentile of page loads, segmented by mobile and desktop. Google states "Core Web Vitals are used by our ranking systems" but "There is no single signal", and "Beyond Core Web Vitals, other page experience aspects don't directly help your website rank higher".

### Cited Findings
- "Core Web Vitals is a set of metrics that measure real-world user experience for loading performance, interactivity, and visual stability of the page. We highly recommend site owners achieve good Core Web Vitals for success with Search and to ensure a great user experience generally." — [Google Search Central: Understanding Core Web Vitals and Google search results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) (LU 2025-12-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Search Central wording of thresholds: "Largest Contentful Paint (LCP): Measures loading performance. ## 7. Structured data general guidelines: JSON-LD placement, JavaScript-injected and late markup, visible-content match, self-serving reviews, spam and manual actions

### Takeaway
JSON-LD is recommended. Google documents it as embedded "in the `<head>` and `<body>` elements", and Google "can read JSON-LD data when it is dynamically injected". Three caveats apply. Rendering "might be skipped" for non-200 pages. `Product` markup should be in the initial HTML. Googlebot reads only the first 2 MB (uncompressed) of an HTML file for Search, so markup beyond that cut-off is never seen. Markup must be "a true representation of the page content" and must not describe invisible content. `LocalBusiness`/`Organization` pages that control their own reviews are ineligible for star reviews. Violations lead to a structured-data manual action, which removes rich-result eligibility but "doesn't affect how the page ranks".

### Cited Findings
- Eligibility: "To be eligible for rich result appearance in Google Search results, structured data shouldn't violate the Content policies for Google Search (which include our spam policies). In addition, this page details the general guidelines that apply to all structured data: they must be followed in order to be eligible for appearance as a rich result in Google Search." — [Google: General structured data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) (LU 2026-07-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Manual action effect: "If your page contains a structured data issue, it can result in a manual action. A structured data manual action means that a page loses eligibility for appearance as a rich result; it doesn't affect how the page ranks in Google web search." — [General SD guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) (LU 2026-07-10 · R 2026-11-12). Also: "If you received a structured data manual action against your page, the structured data on the page will be ignored (although the page can still appear in Google Search results)." — [Organization SD, Troubleshooting](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization) (LU 2026-09-08 · R 2026-11-12)
- No guarantee: "Google does not guarantee that your structured data will show up in search results, even if your page is marked up correctly according to the Rich Results Test." Reasons listed include "The structured data is not representative of the main content of the page, or is potentially misleading." and "The content referred to by the structured data is hidden from the user." — [General SD guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) (LU 2026-07-10 · R 2026-11-12)
- Formats: "In order to be eligible for rich results, mark up your site's pages using one of three supported formats: JSON-LD (recommended), Microdata, RDFa". - JSON-LD placement and injection: "JSON-LD* (Recommended) — A JavaScript notation embedded in a `<script>` tag in the `<head>` and `<body>` elements of an HTML page. ### Inferences
- JSON-LD placed in `<body>` is fine. Its position only matters if it falls beyond the first 2 MB of uncompressed HTML, or if it depends on client-side rendering.
- Server-rendered JSON-LD avoids three documented risks: rendering skipped on non-200 or `noindex` pages, the `Product` crawl-frequency caveat, and the Tag Manager mismatch warning.
- `AggregateRating` or `Review` describing the site owner's own business, nested in its own `LocalBusiness`/`Organization` markup, is ineligible for stars under the current rule. The Manual Actions report also lists "Review written by the site or person providing the service". The 2019 FAQ ("you don't need to remove them") predates that wording, so treat the current docs as controlling.
- Values in markup that never appear on the page (ratings, prices, hours, FAQ answers) are a manual-action risk under "not visible to readers of the page".

### Gaps
- Google states no preference between `<head>` and `<body>` for JSON-LD, and says nothing about JSON-LD "late in the HTML" beyond the 2 MB cut-off.
- The Manual Actions help page shows no last-updated date.

## 8. Google structured data features: required versus recommended properties and eligibility

### Takeaway
- **Organization:** no required properties.
- **LocalBusiness:** requires `address` and `name`.
- **WebSite (site names):** requires `name` and `url`.
- **BreadcrumbList:** requires `itemListElement`, with `ListItem.position`, `name` and `item` (`item` is optional on the last crumb). Shown on desktop only since 22 Jan 2025.
- **Article/BlogPosting:** no required properties.
- **ProfilePage:** requires `mainEntity` (`Person` or `Organization`) with `name`, or `alternateName` as a fallback.
- **Host carousels (`ItemList`):** only for Course list, Movie, Recipe and Restaurant.
- **Structured data carousels (beta):** EEA, Türkiye and South Africa only. Accept `LocalBusiness`, `Product` and `Event` on summary pages with at least three items.
- **Event:** requires `location`, `location.address`, `name` and `startDate`. Since 5 Jun 2025, only physical, publicly bookable events are eligible.
- **Product rich results:** single-product pages only.
- **FAQ rich results:** ended 7 May 2026; docs removed 15 Jun 2026.
- **HowTo rich results:** ended 13 Sep 2023.

#### Article / BlogPosting
- "Adding `Article` structured data to your news, blog, and sports article pages can help Google understand more about the web page and show better title text, images, and date information for the article in search results [...] While there's no markup requirement to be eligible for Google News features like Top stories, you can add `Article` to more explicitly tell Google what your content is about" / "Article objects must be based on one of the following schema.org types: `Article`, `NewsArticle`, `BlogPosting`." / "There are no required properties; instead, add the properties that apply to your content." — [Google: Article structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article) (LU 2026-09-08 · R 2026-11-12)

  - `author` — "`Person` or `Organization` The author of the article."

  - `author.url` — "A link to a web page that uniquely identifies the author of the article. For example, the author's social media page, an "about me" page, or a bio page.   - `headline` — "The title of the article. Consider using a concise title, as long titles may be truncated on some devices."
  - `image` — "Repeated `ImageObject` or `URL` The URL to an image that is representative of the article. Use images that are relevant to the article, rather than logos or captions. ### Inferences
- For a local-business site, the only hard `LocalBusiness` requirements are `address` and `name`. Hours, geo, telephone, url and priceRange are "recommended". `aggregateRating` and `review` about the business itself, on its own site, are ineligible for stars.
- City or category listing pages that mark up several businesses with `ItemList`: classic host carousels do not support generic `LocalBusiness` (only Restaurant, with limited access). The beta carousels do support `LocalBusiness` in the EEA, but require a summary page plus standalone detail pages, at least three items, and `image`, `name` and `url` per item. Google lists interest forms, so eligibility is not automatic (inference).
- The two `priceRange` limits differ: under 100 characters for the `LocalBusiness` knowledge-panel feature, under 12 characters in the beta carousel.
- Breadcrumb markup remains supported but is shown only on desktop since January 2025, so mobile-first audits should not expect breadcrumb rich display.
- `FAQPage` and `HowTo` markup no longer produce any Google rich result. Keeping it is harmless per Google. It is still subject to the general content rules if left on the page (inference). The review-snippet doc still lists `HowTo` as a valid `itemReviewed` type, a leftover.

### Gaps
- Google has no standalone `Person` documentation, so there is no Google list of required `Person` properties outside ProfilePage, Article and Review.
- Google publishes no list of deprecated types that it still parses for "understanding". No primary statement says whether `FAQPage` is used by AI Overviews or AI Mode, beyond "no special schema.org structured data" being needed.
- The `Organization` doc does not say which properties drive the merchant knowledge panel versus behind-the-scenes disambiguation, beyond the examples quoted.

## 9. schema.org definitions as published today (schema.org V30.1, released 2026-09-16)

### Takeaway
schema.org's current `addressCountry` text recommends ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 ("for example "US""), keeps alpha-3 or full names "for backward compatibility", and expects `Country` or `Text`. Google's Organization doc is stricter and asks for alpha-2. Other definitions:
- `logo` expects `ImageObject` or `URL`.
- `sameAs` expects a `URL` "that unambiguously indicates the item's identity".

## 10. Open Graph protocol, X (Twitter) cards, and Google's own use of Open Graph

### Takeaway
- **OGP required:** `og:title`, `og:type`, `og:image` and `og:url`.
- **OGP optional:** `og:description`, `og:locale` (`language_TERRITORY`, default `en_US`), `og:locale:alternate` (array), `og:site_name`, `og:determiner`, `og:audio` and `og:video`.
- **OGP image sub-properties:** `og:image:width`, `og:image:height`, `og:image:alt` ("should" be given whenever `og:image` is), `og:image:type` and `og:image:secure_url`.
- **X card types:** `summary`, `summary_large_image`, `app` and `player`.
- **X fallbacks:** X falls back to OG for title, description, image and image alt.
- **X image limits:** under 5 MB; JPG, PNG, WEBP or GIF; no SVG; 2:1 with a 300×157 minimum for large cards; 1:1 with a 144×144 minimum for summary cards; 4096×4096 maximum; alt text up to 420 characters.
- **X docs status:** the card reference no longer appears in X's live developer docs as of 12 Nov 2026 (old URLs redirect). Archived copies from Dec 2025–Jan 2026 are the latest primary text.
- **Google's use of OG:** Google states it uses `og:title` (title links), `og:site_name` (site names) and `og:image` (preferred thumbnails in Search and Discover). It states nothing about `og:description`, `og:locale`, `og:type` or `og:url`.

### Inferences
- Google documents `og:title`, `og:site_name` and `og:image` as inputs, while its supported-meta-tags page omits all OG tags. Treat that page as incomplete rather than as a statement that OG is ignored.
- `og:locale` and `og:locale:alternate` have no documented Google effect. Google's language detection uses page text, and international targeting relies on hreflang (out of scope here). They remain OGP "optional" properties for other consumers, with the format `language_TERRITORY` (for example `fr_FR` in OGP's own example).
- X falls back to `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image` and `og:image:alt`, so a page with complete OG tags plus `twitter:card` gets a full card. Omitting `twitter:card` "may" yield only a summary card. Large-image cards need a 2:1 image of at least 300×157 that is not SVG and is under 5 MB.

### Gaps
- X no longer hosts a live cards markup reference, so I cannot confirm that the archived limits (5 MB, 4096 px, 420-character alt, 70/200-character title/description) still apply on 12 Nov 2026. The latest primary text is the January 2026 archive.
- No primary Google statement covers `og:description`, `og:type`, `og:url`, `og:locale` or `og:image:width/height/alt`.
- ogp.me shows no version or last-updated date.

### Inferences
- From the 12 Jun 2025 and 5 Nov 2025 posts, Google's direction is to retire low-usage rich results while continuing to support core types (Organization, LocalBusiness, Article, Breadcrumb on desktop, Product, Review, Event, ProfilePage, carousels). Google has stated that removals do not affect ranking for the June 2025 batch, and for FAQ and HowTo in 2023 ("should not be considered a ranking change").
- None of the 2025–2026 removals requires deleting markup ("Unsupported structured data like this won't cause issues in Search"). An audit should reclassify such markup from "rich-result opportunity" to "optional/no Google display".
